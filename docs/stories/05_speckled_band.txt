The Adventure of the Speckled Band

Early one morning, Dr. Watson is awakened by Sherlock Holmes. A
frightened young woman, Helen Stoner, has arrived at Baker Street.

Helen lives at Stoke Moran with her stepfather, Dr. Grimesby Roylott, a
former physician who lived for years in India. Roylott is violent,
physically powerful, and fascinated by exotic animals.

Helen tells Holmes that her twin sister Julia died mysteriously two
years earlier.

Shortly before Julia’s wedding, she began hearing a low whistle at
night. One evening she rushed from her locked bedroom and collapsed in
the corridor.

Her final words were: “The band! The speckled band!”

No intruder was found. The windows were secured and the bedroom door had
been locked from inside.

Now Helen herself is engaged. Repairs have suddenly forced her to sleep
in Julia’s old bedroom, and she has begun hearing the same whistle.

Holmes questions her about the house, Roylott’s finances, and the
sisters’ inheritance. He learns that Roylott would lose part of his
income when either daughter married.

After Helen leaves, Roylott storms into Baker Street and warns Holmes to
stay away. He bends a metal fireplace poker to demonstrate his strength.
After Roylott leaves, Holmes calmly straightens it.

Holmes and Watson later inspect Stoke Moran.

Several details trouble Holmes. A ventilation opening in Julia’s old
room does not lead outdoors; it opens into Roylott’s room. A bell rope
hangs beside the bed but connects to no bell. The bed is fixed to the
floor.

In Roylott’s room Holmes finds a safe, a saucer of milk, and a looped
leash.

That night Helen secretly leaves the bedroom while Holmes and Watson
wait inside it in darkness.

Hours pass.

A faint light appears through the ventilator. They smell heated metal.
Then comes the soft whistle.

Something begins moving down the useless bell rope.

Holmes strikes at it with his cane.

A terrible scream erupts from the next room.

They rush into Roylott’s chamber and find him dead. Around his head is
what first appears to be a spotted band.

Then it moves.

It is a venomous snake.

Holmes reconstructs the crime. Roylott had obtained the snake in India.
He sent it through the ventilator at night. The bell rope gave it a path
to the bed, and the bed had been fixed in place so the victim would
always sleep beneath it. The whistle summoned the snake back, while the
milk helped train it.

Julia’s dying description of a “speckled band” had been literal, but
incomplete.

When Holmes struck the snake, it returned through the ventilator in an
agitated state and bit Roylott.

The mystery was solved not by a single dramatic clue, but by
contradictions: a ventilator that ventilated nothing, a bell rope that
rang no bell, a bed that could not move, and a man who possessed motive,
access, and an unusual weapon.
