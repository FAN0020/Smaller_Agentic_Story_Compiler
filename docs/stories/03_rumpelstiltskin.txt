Rumpelstiltskin

A miller wished to appear important, so he boasted to the king that his
daughter could spin straw into gold.

The king loved gold. He ordered the daughter brought to the palace and
locked her in a room filled with straw.

“By morning, this must be gold.”

The girl began to cry. She possessed no such power.

Near midnight a strange little man appeared.

“What will you give me if I spin the straw into gold?”

She offered her necklace. The little man worked through the night, and
by dawn the straw had become shining thread.

The king was delighted but greedy. He placed her in a larger room and
demanded the same miracle. The little man returned, accepted her ring,
and again filled the room with gold.

On the third night the girl had nothing left.

“Promise me your first child when you become queen,” said the little
man.

Desperate, she agreed.

The king married her. In time she had a child, and the little man
returned to claim it.

The queen offered gold, jewels, and land. He refused, but finally gave
her one chance.

“You have three days. If you discover my name, you may keep the child.”

The queen sent messengers throughout the kingdom to gather names.

On the first night she guessed common names. None was correct.

On the second she tried rare and ridiculous names. The little man
laughed at every attempt.

On the third day a messenger returned from a distant forest. He had seen
a tiny house and, outside it, a little man dancing around a fire and
singing:

“Tomorrow I take the royal child; no one knows my secret name.”

In the song he had revealed the name.

That night the little man appeared confidently.

“Is your name Henry?” asked the queen.

“No.”

“Felix?”

“No!”

“Then perhaps your name is Rumpelstiltskin.”

The little man froze.

His secret had been discovered. Furious, he stamped the floor, shouted,
and fled from the palace.

The queen held her child close. She had escaped the price of a desperate
promise, while the miller’s original boast remained a warning about
claims made without thought.
