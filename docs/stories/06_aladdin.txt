Aladdin and the Wonderful Lamp

Aladdin is a poor young man living with his widowed mother. He is clever
but undisciplined and has little interest in learning a trade.

A stranger arrives claiming to be Aladdin’s uncle. He is actually a
magician searching for a hidden lamp that only Aladdin can retrieve.

The magician wins the family’s trust, buys Aladdin fine clothes, and
promises him a prosperous future. Then he leads him beyond the city,
performs an incantation, and opens a passage in the earth.

He gives Aladdin a protective ring and orders him to enter the
underground chamber. Deep inside, Aladdin finds a garden whose trees
bear glittering objects that he assumes are colored glass. He fills his
pockets with them and finds the old lamp.

At the entrance, the magician demands the lamp before helping Aladdin
out.

Aladdin refuses.

Enraged, the magician seals the entrance and abandons him underground.

In despair Aladdin rubs the ring. A supernatural servant appears and
carries him home.

Later, while cleaning the old lamp, Aladdin’s mother releases a far more
powerful genie. The genie of the lamp can provide food, riches,
buildings, and almost anything commanded of it.

Aladdin also discovers that the colored stones from the underground
garden are priceless jewels.

As he grows older, he sees Princess Badroulbadour and wishes to marry
her. His mother presents the sultan with jewels. Their beauty is so
extraordinary that the court is astonished.

Through the lamp, Aladdin creates wealth and a magnificent palace. He
eventually marries the princess.

But the magician learns that Aladdin survived.

He returns disguised as a merchant, walking through the streets and
offering “new lamps for old.” The princess does not know the secret of
Aladdin’s battered lamp and allows a servant to exchange it.

Now controlling the genie, the magician orders Aladdin’s entire palace,
with the princess inside, transported to a distant land.

Aladdin returns to find empty ground.

He no longer has the lamp, but he still possesses the magic ring. Its
genie cannot simply undo the stronger genie’s command, but it can carry
Aladdin to the stolen palace.

Aladdin reunites with the princess. Together they plan to defeat the
magician rather than confront him openly. The princess pretends to
accept the magician’s victory and invites him to dine. While his guard
is lowered, he is overcome, and Aladdin recovers the lamp.

The genie returns the palace to its original place.

The story does not end with the first victory. A relative of the
magician later attempts revenge through disguise and deception. Aladdin
must again recognize that magical power cannot replace judgment.

Across the story, the lamp changes Aladdin’s circumstances, but
possession of the lamp alone never guarantees safety. The object
repeatedly changes hands. Trust, secrecy, identity, and knowledge of the
lamp’s rules are as important as raw magical power.

Aladdin ultimately keeps the palace and his family, having changed from
an idle youth into someone capable of surviving enemies who understand
his greatest advantage.
