The Hare and the Tortoise

A hare was famous among the animals for his speed and rarely allowed
anyone to forget it.

“No one here can race me,” he boasted.

A tortoise quietly answered, “Then race me.”

The animals laughed, but a course was chosen.

At the signal, the hare shot forward. Soon the tortoise was far behind.

“This is hardly a race,” said the hare. “I have time for a nap.”

He lay beneath a tree and fell asleep.

The tortoise continued. He did not hurry, because he could not. He
simply kept moving: one step, another step, and another.

Eventually he passed the sleeping hare.

The cheering of the animals woke the hare. He sprang up and ran as fast
as he could, but the tortoise crossed the finish first.

The hare arrived breathless and astonished.

The tortoise said, “Speed is useful. Continuing is useful too.”

Moral: Steady effort can defeat careless talent.
